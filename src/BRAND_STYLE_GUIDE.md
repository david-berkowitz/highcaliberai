# High Caliber AI — Brand & Content Style Guide
*For use in Base44 and all High Caliber AI content.*

---

## 1. What High Caliber AI Is

High Caliber AI is David Berkowitz's consulting and thought leadership platform focused on applied AI for marketing and business. It is the professional home for his consulting practice, his book, his speaking, and his content.

It is **not** a community or a media brand. It is a practitioner-led practice built on deep expertise, real-world application, and a strong point of view on how AI actually works for marketers and business leaders.

The name reflects a precise, considered approach to AI, not hype.

---

David Berkowitz is the founder of High Caliber AI. His background and identity inform how the brand sounds and what it stands for.

**Current roles**
- Founder, High Caliber AI

- Thought leadership advisor at Intuit Mailchimp

**What he's known for**

- A career spanning agency leadership (360i, MRY, iCrossing), tech companies (Mediaocean, eMarketer), and independent consulting
- A connector: someone who builds real relationships and opens doors for others

- ✅ Correct: "David founded AI Marketers Guild, one of the leading communities for AI-focused marketers."

## 3. Audience

High Caliber AI speaks to a mix of senior decision-makers across marketing and business:

- CMOs and marketing leaders at mid-size and large companies
- Startup founders who need to move fast and make smart AI bets
- Agency executives navigating AI adoption for themselves and their clients
- SMB owners looking for practical, accessible guidance

The common thread: they're intelligent, busy, and skeptical of hype. They want to know what actually works and how to put it to use.

**Do not write down to the audience. Do not over-explain. Assume they're smart.**

---

## 4. Voice and Tone

High Caliber AI sounds like David: a senior practitioner who has seen a lot, knows a lot, and communicates with clarity and confidence. Smart without being academic. Helpful without being a pushover. Occasionally funny without trying too hard.

**Core voice qualities**
- **Helpful:** The goal is always to make the reader smarter or better equipped.
- **Practical:** Ground everything in what actually happens in the real world.
- **Conversational:** Write like you'd talk to a smart colleague, not like a press release.
- **Clear:** Say the thing directly. No hedging, no filler.
- **Expert:** The expertise is evident through specificity, not through self-promotion.
- **Connector-oriented:** Writing reflects an awareness that ideas and relationships go together.
- **A bit fun:** Dry wit and occasional humor are welcome. They're part of the brand.

**Tone calibration by format**
- Homepage and about copy: Confident, direct, slightly warm. No sales-y language.
- Blog posts: Conversational, point-of-view-driven, useful. First person is fine.
- Service descriptions: Clear and specific. What it is, who it's for, what they get.
- Case studies: Let the work speak. Lead with outcomes, not process.
- Bios: Concise. Focus on relevance to the context, not the full resume.
- Speaking pitches: Direct and confident. Why David, why this topic, why now.

## 5. Language

**Always**
- Active voice
- Plain language over industry jargon
- Specific over vague (name the tool, the tactic, the result)
- Short sentences when the point is sharp
- "High Caliber AI" (two words, both capitalized)

**Never**
- "Leverage" (use "use")
- "Unlock" as a verb for capabilities
- "Cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," or "revolutionary"
- Em dashes unless unavoidable
- Emoji in professional content
- Hashtags in newsletters or long-form content
- Random bolding for emphasis — bold is for headers or genuinely critical content only
- "Hope you're doing well" or similar filler openers

**Preferred phrasing**
- "AI marketing" not "marketing AI"
- "Applied AI" when referring to practical, real-world use
- "Fractional execs" not "fractional CXOs"
- "*The Non-Obvious Guide to AI Marketing*" for the full book title
- "AI Marketers Guild" — never "the AI Marketers Guild" (unless used as a descriptor, e.g., "the AI Marketers Guild community")

---

## 6. Formatting

- Lead with the most important point, not context or background
- Keep paragraphs short — two to four sentences as a default
- Bullets for lists; prose for arguments
- No section breaks unless the piece is explicitly structured as a report or guide
- Normal bullet formatting only — no checkmarks, arrows, or emoji bullets

## 7. Positioning

High Caliber AI's positioning is built on three things: David's depth of expertise, his record of applying AI to real marketing challenges, and his ability to make complex ideas actionable.

The positioning is **not** built on access to a network or community. It is built on what David knows and what he can do.

**What to emphasize**
- Practical, applied expertise — not theory
- A strong point of view — not neutral "here are the options" consulting
- Results and specifics — not vague promises
- David's range: he has been a CMO, an agency leader, a tech exec, an author, and a speaker. He has seen the problem from every side.

**What to avoid**
- Positioning the community as a client benefit
- Over-indexing on credentials at the expense of showing the work
- AI hype language that sounds like every other AI brand

David founded AI Marketers Guild and built it into a major community for AI-focused marketers. It now operates under Marketecture as part of David's role as Chief Community Officer. This is an achievement and a credential. It is not a resource clients access through High Caliber AI.

## 9. Bios and About Copy

Bios should be calibrated to context. Always use the shortest version that works for the context.

**Short bio (1–2 sentences)**
David Berkowitz is the founder of High Caliber AI and the author of *The Non-Obvious Guide to AI Marketing* (Ideapress, 2025). He helps marketers and business leaders put AI to practical use.

**Medium bio (one paragraph)**
David Berkowitz is the founder of High Caliber AI, a consulting and thought leadership practice focused on applied AI for marketing. He is the author of *The Non-Obvious Guide to AI Marketing* (Ideapress, 2025) and serves as Chief Community Officer at Marketecture. With more than 1,000 bylines and 400+ speaking engagements, he has spent his career helping marketers work smarter — across agency roles at 360i and MRY, tech companies including Mediaocean and eMarketer, and now as an independent consultant and advisor.

**Notes on bios**
- Lead with High Caliber AI and the book — those are the current anchors.
- Include community credentials (AI Marketers Guild, Serial Marketers) in longer bios as context.
- Tailor to the audience: for a marketing event, lead with the marketing angle; for a tech audience, lean into the applied AI expertise.
- Keep the tone confident and human — not a LinkedIn punchlist.

## 10. Content Types and Guidance

**Blog posts**
First person. Conversational but substantive. Open with a hook — a specific observation, a question, or a counterintuitive take. End with a clear takeaway, not a generic CTA. Length: as long as the idea needs. Most posts are 400–800 words.

**Service descriptions**
Lead with who it is for and what they get. Be specific about what is included and what the outcome looks like. No buzzwords. No vague capability claims.

**Case studies**
Lead with the outcome. Show the thinking, not just the result. Keep the client's voice present where possible.

**Speaking pitches**
Why David. Why this topic. Why this audience. Why now. Concrete and confident. Not a list of credentials. Sign off with "Thanks" not "Best regards" or similar.

**Newsletter content**
No hashtags. Concise. Respect the reader's time. One clear thread per issue, not a grab-bag.

---

## 11. The Gut Check

When in doubt: Would David say this? Is it specific? Is it useful? If yes to all three, you're probably good.
